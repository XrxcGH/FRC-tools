# Adversarial Review: Courier / Season Pack / Ledger

Verified against `00-strategy.md` and `02-section-data.md`, plus live checks of the 2026 FRC Game Manual, the 2027 season calendar, Apple's fee-waiver policy, Capacitor releases, ChromeOS AUE data, and published BLE throughput measurements.

**What survives scrutiny:** E301 is real, is §14.3 of the 2026 manual, reads exactly as quoted, and does **not** mention Bluetooth or 2.4 GHz (the FTC manual's identically-numbered E301 *does* — the doc dodged a landmine it never mentions). The blue box on hotspots is real. R905 scoping to the OPERATOR CONSOLE checks out. Web Bluetooth peripheral-role absence and iOS Safari absence are correct. The contrast arithmetic in Frontend §9 is correct to three digits (17.3:1, 0.103, 0.530, 3.78:1). The envelope size math (~420 B, 302 kB/day, 25 s) is correct. Capacitor 8.5 exists. That's the good news, and it ends here.

## (a) Prioritized defects

### P0 — These make the document unbuildable as written

**1. [Cross] There are four mutually incompatible Courier envelope definitions.** Product FR-1: `{envelope_id, event_key, match_key?, team_key?, scout_id, schema_id, created_at, content_hash, producer_key_id, sig}`. Backend §3.1: a 16-key CBOR map with `mt` (packed uint), `tm` (uint), `hlc`, `bh`, `bz`, `enc`, `rev`, `sup`, `tid`, `mid` — and no `envelope_id`. Frontend §2.2: `{contentHash, eventKey, matchKey, teamKey, scoutId, schemaId, revision, sealedAt, body, sig, deviceKey, replicas}` — no HLC, no `tid`, no `mid`, no `sup`, plus a `replicas` field that exists nowhere else. SecOps §3 `admit()`: `env.content_hash`, `env.origin_pk`, `env.codec`, `env.submitted_at` — a fourth naming scheme. Four teams reading this build four incompatible wire formats. **Fix:** delete the envelope from Product FR-1 and Frontend §2.2, replace both with a normative pointer to a single CDDL in Backend §3.1, and regenerate the SecOps validator signature from it. Publish the CDDL as a versioned file in-repo before any code.

**2. [Product FR-2 / Frontend §2.2] Dedup by body content hash silently destroys the 10% double-scouting signal — the thing the whole analytics layer depends on for identifiability.** FR-2 says "deduplicate by `content_hash`"; Frontend makes `contentHash` (explicitly "BLAKE3 of canonical CBOR **body**") the IndexedDB primary key. Two scouts watching the same robot in a low-event match will frequently produce byte-identical bodies. One is dropped. Meanwhile Backend §4 asserts "Different `sid` → **both survive**", and Data §7 says deliberate double-scouting is "the cheapest identifiability lever" for the EM scout-reliability model. Frontend's own `enqueue` compounds this: a scout who goes A → B → A produces `hash(A)` a third time, and `db.envelope.put(env)` **overwrites the original row with `revision: 2`**, destroying the audit chain the document swears is append-only. **Fix:** dedup key is `record_id = BLAKE3(canonical CBOR of the whole record)` everywhere, per Backend §3.1. `bh` stays as an integrity field only. Change FR-2 and the Dexie primary key. Add `sup` to the Frontend interface.

**3. [Product FR-4 vs Backend §3.5] A functional requirement mandates a mechanism the architecture explicitly rejects.** FR-4: "Converge two divergent logs... **via rateless IBLT set reconciliation**. *Test:* 2,000 vs 2,050 envelopes converge in < 8 KB." Journey (a)'s sequence diagram shows "IBLT sketch exchange (< 8 KB)". Backend §3.5: "**Rejected: IBLT-based set reconciliation** — sizing `d` requires estimating the symmetric difference in advance." Backend ships hierarchical range digests instead. **Fix:** rewrite FR-4 as an outcome ("converge in ≤ 2 round-trips and ≤ 8 KB for a 50-envelope symmetric difference"), delete the mechanism, fix the mermaid diagram.

**4. [Cross] Nothing in the document designs the decoder, and without it every analytics feature is dead.** Courier bodies are opaque by construction. Data §4's constrained blend, Data §7's D1–D5 detectors, Frontend §6's uncertainty charts, Frontend §10's `useDecoder(c.schemaId)`, and the entire picklist all consume *decoded* values. Frontend §4 states the form engine "produces one only the team's own decoder reads." **No section specifies where a decoder comes from, how `schema_id` resolves to one, who writes it, or what happens when it doesn't exist.** Season Pack describes *official* scoring only — it cannot decode a team's scouting body. The result: a team gets transport, and then has to write a bespoke decoder to get any value from Ledger, Data §4, or half the frontend. This is the single largest hand-wave in the document. **Fix:** either (a) define a `schema_id` → decoder registry with a reference JSON-Schema-driven decoder generated from the same `form.schema.json` Pack Studio emits, shipped as `@courier/decode`, or (b) scope Ledger to official data only and delete every feature that touches a scouted per-robot value. Pick one and say so in §4 Goals.

**5. [Product Journey (c) / Data §4] Saturday's official alliance totals cannot reach the pit, so the constrained blend is inoperative exactly when the picklist is made.** Journey (c): venue pack pulled Friday 22:00, "no internet all day", then "blends them against official alliance totals as a constrained update" at 17:45 Saturday. The constrained update `Ax = b` requires `b` = the official alliance total *for each match played that day*. FR-24 refreshes venue packs *nightly*. Courier moves scouting envelopes, not FMS results. There is no designed path for Saturday's official scores to reach an offline pit. Data §7's hard arithmetic-impossibility gate and Frontend §10's `sum_exceeds_official` ConflictCard have the same dependency and the same hole. **Fix:** name the path explicitly. The only honest ones are: one designated runner leaves the venue hourly with a phone on cellular and re-injects a signed `courier.results.v1` payload into the mesh, or the blend degrades to unconstrained (prior + scouts only) with the constraint applied Saturday night. Write the degraded mode into Data §4 and add an `official_totals_as_of_match` field to the staleness banner.

**6. [SecOps §1 vs Backend §3.1] Cross-team pseudonymization is mathematically impossible in a signed, content-addressed record.** SecOps §1: "egress rewrites it: `scout_id_out = BLAKE3(scout_id ‖ event_key ‖ alliance_secret)[0:8]`." But `sid` is field 5 of `CourierRecord`, which is the COSE_Sign1 payload, and `record_id = BLAKE3(canonical CBOR of CourierRecord)`. Rewriting `sid` invalidates the Ed25519 signature and changes the record identity — so the partner receives an unverifiable envelope with a new `record_id` that will double-count against the original if it ever loops back. **Fix:** move `sid` out of the signed record into a per-mesh side table, and put a *per-event* signed pseudonym in the record from the start (`sid_event = BLAKE3(sid ‖ event_key ‖ mesh_key)`), minted at seal time. Then egress is a no-op and cross-event linkage is broken by construction — which is what §1 actually wanted.

**7. [Backend §5] The `enc: 2` body encryption provides zero confidentiality, because the plaintext body hash ships in the clear next to it.** Field 9 `bh` = "BLAKE3-256 of body" lives in the cleartext CourierRecord. Field 12 `body` is encrypted under the mesh key when `enc: 2`. Any relay operator or eavesdropper who can guess a scouting body — and the space is small: a handful of integers and booleans bounded by the Season Pack's `max_plausible_per_match` — confirms it by hashing. This is a total break of the stated goal ("teams unwilling to let a relay operator read envelopes"). The nonce derivation `BLAKE3(mesh_key_id ‖ bh)[0..24]` makes it worse by binding the nonce to a public value. **Fix:** when `enc != 0`, `bh` must be the hash of the *ciphertext*, and dedup must key on ciphertext. Then state plainly that cross-app dedup in the Bridge is impossible for encrypted envelopes, and accept it.

**8. [SecOps §2 T7 vs Backend §4/§6] The replay defense destroys CRDT convergence and references a field that does not exist.** T7: "Envelopes additionally carry `(event_key, seq)` per `scout_id`; a receiver rejects `seq` below its high-water mark." There is no `seq` in the Backend CBOR map. Worse, a high-water-mark rejection is fatal in epidemic gossip: out-of-order arrival is the *normal* case with fanout-2 anti-entropy, so a device that hears match 60 before match 55 permanently drops match 55, and §6's formal claim ("for any interleaving of pair/disconnect events, the union converges") becomes false. **Fix:** delete the seq rule. T7 is already mitigated — replay of a valid signed record into a grow-only set is genuinely idempotent, which the same table admits one sentence earlier.

**9. [Backend §3.4 vs SecOps §4 C2] The architecture's #1 fallback transport is the thing the compliance gate warns against.** Backend §3.4 ranks "**USB tethering (RNDIS)**" as the top legal fallback. SecOps C2: "The app never prompts to enable tethering. On Android it detects active tethering and shows a rules warning." Both cannot be true. Separately, the technical claim is wrong on iOS: enabling Personal Hotspot to get USB tethering also brings up the Wi-Fi AP unless the user manually disables "Allow Others to Join" — the design's preferred fallback ships an E301 violation as a default side effect. It also requires an active cellular data plan on most Android OEM builds, and two phones cannot tether to each other at all. **Fix:** delete USB tethering. Promote mass-storage sneakernet (Backend §3.4 item 2) to the primary USB path — it is what 3128 actually retreated to, it is what the Product section cites, and it has no rules surface at all.

**10. [Product FR-3 vs Frontend §2.3 vs NFR-6] Non-exportable platform-keystore keys are impossible on the ChromeOS/web target the design makes first-class.** FR-3 mandates "a non-exportable Ed25519 keypair in the platform keystore." Frontend's own code does `ed25519.sign(bytes, await deviceSecretKey())` — a raw secret key handed to JavaScript. On an AUE'd Chromebook (see defect 21: frozen mid-2022, ~Chrome 102), WebCrypto has no Ed25519 at all, so the key is a `@noble` software key sitting in IndexedDB, fully exportable, on an unpatched browser. Open question 10 worries about Android API 33; it misses the platform where the guarantee is simply unavailable. **Also:** that same line signs **only the body bytes**, leaving `event_key`, `match`, `team`, `scout_id`, and `schema_id` unsigned and freely tamperable — directly contradicting FR-3, COSE_Sign1, and every trust claim in Backend §3.2. **Fix:** downgrade FR-3 to "hardware-backed where the platform provides it, software-backed otherwise, and the envelope records which," surface the distinction in the keyring op, and fix the code sample to sign the canonical record.

**11. [SecOps §7 gantt] The release calendar is wrong by six weeks and contradicts the verified 2027 kickoff.** The gantt places the kickoff sprint at `01-03` and "Weeks 1-6 regionals/districts" at `01-10, 45d`. FIRST has published 2027 kickoff as **January 9, 2027** (BIOCORE, Houston Championship) — which the Product section states correctly. Week 1 events cannot start January 10, one day after kickoff; with a ~6.5-week build season they land in **late February**. The pre-kickoff freeze (`12-20, 14d`) also ends six days before the real kickoff. Every freeze window in the document's operational plan is misaligned with reality. **Fix:** rebuild the gantt from the published 2027 calendar: kickoff 01-09, freeze 12-26→01-09, Week 1 ≈ 02-24, Weeks 1-6 through ≈ 04-10, district champs ≈ 04-07→04-17, Championship ≈ 04-21.

**12. [Product FR-15/§7 vs SecOps §5/§11] The Season Pack deadline — the one metric the document calls "terminal on one miss" — is defined two different ways, six weeks apart.** Product FR-15 and §7: `1.0.0` within **72 h of kickoff**, "published by 2027-01-12T23:59Z". SecOps §5 SLO: "Season Pack published after **first official match data** ≤72 h." SecOps §11 P2 kill criterion: "**Not published within 72 h of first official match data.**" These are the same phase, the same artifact, and roughly a six-week gap. Open question 2 flags exactly this and it was never resolved before the kill criterion was written. **Fix:** two separate, separately-measured commitments — `1.0.0` (manual-derived) at K+72h, `1.1.0` (FMS-bound) at B+48h — each with its own SLO row and its own kill criterion. And answer open question 2 first, because if no consumer can use an unbound pack, the January headline is marketing and the real gate is Week 1.

### P1 — Contradictions that will produce a wrong build

**13. [NFR-2 vs Backend §3.3 vs Frontend §2.3] Three incompatible gossip schedules; the backend's own arithmetic violates the product's latency NFR.** NFR-2: envelope on collector ≤90 s. Product journey: "adaptive 1-4 s" advertising. Backend §3.3: "anti-entropy runs every 45 s with fanout 2... **~180 s to full propagation**." Frontend: `setInterval(drain, 90_000)` — a 90 s round timer, so the *first attempt* alone can consume the entire NFR-2 budget. **Fix:** pick one. If 90 s is the requirement, the anti-entropy interval must be ≤20 s with fanout 3, and the backend must redo the ⌈log₃ N⌉ calculation and re-derive the battery number in NFR-1, which is currently computed for a 1 s advertising interval and no sync bursts.

**14. [NFR-3/NFR-4 vs Backend §3.3 vs SecOps C3] Four different BLE throughput numbers, one of them labeled "measured" when open question 9 admits nothing was measured.** NFR-3: "~25 kB/s sustained" on a mixed fleet. NFR-4: 28 MB at 25 kB/s. Backend §3.3: iOS-peripheral GATT design number **12 kB/s**, L2CAP raw ~110 kB/s / design 60 kB/s. SecOps C3: L2CAP "**measured at** 30–90 kB/s". At the backend's own 12 kB/s, NFR-3's cold-catch-up budget is 9.2 s of payload, not 4.4 s, and NFR-4's venue-pack estimate goes from 19 to 39 minutes. Published iOS peripheral measurements put realistic ceilings at 300–400 kbps (~37–50 kB/s) *before* the well-documented `updateValue` backpressure problem, where the second and subsequent notifications return `false` and the `peripheralManagerIsReadyToUpdateSubscribers` callback stalls 50–100 ms. That backpressure, not the frame math, is what will determine real iOS throughput and it is not modeled anywhere. **Fix:** one number per path in one table in Backend §3.3, referenced by every NFR. Change "measured" to "estimated — see open question 9" in C3. Add the `updateValue` backpressure to the iOS section as a named risk with a credit-window mitigation.

**15. [Backend §3.3] L2CAP CoC is unjustified machinery, and the stated justification is factually wrong.** "A stream socket with **no ATT overhead**" — ATT overhead with data-length extension is 3 bytes against a 244-byte payload, ~1.2%. Published guidance is explicit that the throughput difference between L2CAP CoC and GATT-with-DLE is small in practice. Meanwhile the section's own conclusion is "**bandwidth is not the constraint**", and NFR-4 routes everything over 1 MB *off* BLE entirely. So the design ships a second full transport state machine, on two platforms, for a workload that by its own analysis takes 25 seconds a day on the slow path. This is exactly the cross-platform native maintenance labor the document identifies as what killed every predecessor. **Fix:** cut L2CAP CoC from v1. Put it in v2 behind a measured requirement. The real justification (escaping per-notification API backpressure) may be valid — but then say that, and measure it in P0.

**16. [Backend §8 vs SecOps §8 vs NFR-8] Three incompatible cost models, one of which breaches the product's own ceiling.** NFR-8: "≤ **$250 per season**", with "~$60 object storage" on "R2-class zero-egress storage". Backend §8: no R2 at all — GitHub Releases + Cloudflare Pages + Hugging Face + Zenodo, "Total unavoidable recurring cost: **$99/year**." SecOps §8: Cloudflare R2 + a Hetzner CX32, totaling **$128 / $238 / $516**. The 3,500-team column breaks NFR-8 by 2×. The runbook (SecOps §5) then instructs the operator to roll back `index.jsonl` "via the **R2 web UI**" — a bucket that Backend §8 doesn't provision. **Fix:** one hosting topology, one cost table, in Backend §8. SecOps §8 references it. Delete the Hetzner box or justify it against "GitHub Actions, $0."

**17. [Backend §8 vs Constraint 17 / FR-32] Primary distribution is on the host that constraint 17 says is blocked.** Backend §8 puts hot artifacts on GitHub Releases and all compute on GitHub Actions. FR-32 requires a non-GitHub mirror because "school districts block GitHub." SecOps §5 runbook step 3 even routes the status page off GitHub "*because one mentor's school blocks GitHub*." So the design knows, and still made GitHub the primary. **Fix:** CDN domain is primary for artifacts; GitHub is the mirror. Actions can stay (it's CI, not distribution), but the release publish step must write to the CDN first.

**18. [NFR-1 vs Frontend §5 vs SecOps C8] Three battery budgets that cannot all be met.** NFR-1: transport overhead ≤350 mWh over **12 h** on a Pixel 6a. Frontend §5: "Battery ≤ **6%/hr**, screen on" → 48% over 8 h. SecOps C8: "a full **8-hour** event day of sync on an **iPhone SE (2020)** at ≤**22%**." An iPhone SE 2020 is a 6.96 Wh battery; 22% is ~1,530 mWh, i.e. 4.4× the NFR-1 allowance, while Frontend's own 6%/hr budget blows past 22% by hour four. Three different reference devices, three different day lengths, three incompatible ceilings. **Fix:** one gate, on one device, for one duration, split into transport-overhead and screen-on components, and delete the other two.

**19. [NFR-6 vs Frontend §5 / §3] Memory budgets contradict, and the analysis route as specified will OOM the named reference device.** NFR-6: "heap ≤ **120 MB** with 20,000 envelopes." Frontend §5: "≤90 MB scouting; ≤**320 MB** analysis with a 40 MB pack." Frontend §3 rejects DuckDB-WASM as "unsurvivable on a 3 GB Fire tablet" and then loads a 12–40 MB SQLite into **sql.js MEMFS** — meaning the entire file is resident in the wasm heap, plus query working set, plus a 1.2 MB wasm module, on a Chromium-90-class WebView with a ~2 GB device ceiling and per-tab renderer limits. **Fix:** one memory budget per route in one place; and specify pack *streaming* (range-read a paged SQLite, or split the pack into a small L2 summary the venue route uses and a full pack the laptop uses). Measure before committing.

**20. [Product FR-7 vs Frontend §2–§3] "≤ 30 lines and zero schema change" is not achievable given the frontend design.** FR-7's acceptance test is a QRScout fork integrating in ≤30 LOC. Frontend requires the host app to adopt a Dexie 4 database named `courier` with seven tables, an outbox hook with its own drain loop and backoff table, a hand-written `injectManifest` service worker with a manual update gate keyed to `eventMode`, and `@courier/ui` components. Also, the plugin API differs between sections: FR-7 says `publish() / subscribe() / status() / exportBundle()`; the frontend calls `Courier.push()`, `Courier.bestTransport()`, `Courier.addListener()`. **Fix:** define a genuinely 30-line surface — `Courier.publish(meta, bytes)` and one `<OutboxPill/>` — with the Dexie schema owned entirely inside the plugin and invisible to the host. Then actually write the QRScout fork in v0; it is the single most load-bearing acceptance test in the document.

**21. [Cross] Three mutually exclusive pairing schemes.** Product FR-10: "a ≤ **6-digit** event-scoped code." Backend §3.2: QR invite + Ed25519 keys + a **6-digit numeric SAS**, explicitly rejecting a printed team secret. SecOps §2 T1: QR carrying an **X25519** public key + 128-bit session secret, with a **10-char Crockford base32 (50-bit)** typed fallback, explicitly rejecting "6-digit PINs — 20 bits against an attacker sitting 4 m away all day." Backend never mentions X25519 or Noise; SecOps T6 mandates "Noise `XK` over an L2CAP CoC" that Backend §3.3 does not describe. Backend's device keys are Ed25519; SecOps pins X25519 — no conversion is specified. **Fix:** one ceremony, in Backend §3.2, normative. If Noise XK is the transport, it must appear in the GATT/L2CAP framing section, not only in a threat table. If both key types are needed, state the Ed25519→X25519 derivation.

**22. [Product FR-19 vs Backend §6 vs SecOps §9] Signing threshold is 1-of-2 in two places and 2-of-N in a third.** FR-19: "2 signers / **any 1 signs**." Data §3 role table: "Release Signer 2 (**any 1 signs**)." Backend §6: "Release signing is **2-of-N** Ed25519 detached signatures." SecOps §9 adds a Shamir 3-of-5 root on top. **Fix:** 1-of-2 for a 72-hour kickoff sprint (2-of-2 will miss the deadline the first time a signer is on a plane); 2-of-2 for MAJOR versions. Say it once.

**23. [Product FR-9 vs FR-1] Exporting opaque bodies to CSV requires parsing them.** FR-1: Courier "MUST NOT parse, validate, transform, or log the body." FR-9: "Export bundles as `.ndjson`, `.csv`, and a raw concatenation matching each source app's own importer." A CSV of opaque CBOR blobs is not a CSV, and "matching each source app's own importer" means Courier knows each app's format — the exact coupling FR-8 and the whole opacity thesis exist to avoid. **Fix:** FR-9 exports envelope *metadata* as CSV and bodies as length-prefixed raw. Per-app importer formats are the Bridge's `bridge_profiles.json` problem, not the transport's, and should be labeled as such.

**24. [SecOps §4] E302 is never analyzed, and it is the rule that actually bites the cross-team relay.** The 2026 manual's E302 reads: *"Participants may not interfere, attempt to interfere, or attempt to connect with any other team or FIRST wireless communication except as expressly allowed for purposes of communicating with their team's ROBOT..."* FR-13's cross-team opt-in relay is, on a plain reading, students' devices attempting to connect to another team's wireless communication. The document reviews E301 and R905 exhaustively and never mentions E302. An FTA will. **Fix:** add E302 to the C-table, add the mutual-consent argument, and put E302 — not just E301 — in the FR-14 rules memo and the FTA review. This is also the strongest argument yet for the FR-14 FTA review happening *before* P0 ends, not before v1.

**25. [Product §4] "No WiFi... of any kind. E301, venue-wide" misstates the rule.** E301 bans teams *setting up their own* 802.11. It does not ban using a venue-provided network or cellular. The non-goal is a defensible product decision (venue uplinks saturate — constraint 4) but it is stated as a rules constraint, and that is wrong. It matters because it is the framing the FTA memo will be judged on, and because the doc has spent its credibility on rules accuracy. **Fix:** split the non-goal into "E301 forbids us creating a network" and "we choose not to depend on any network we did not create, because constraint 4."

### P2 — Factual errors, drift, and machinery with no requirement

**26. [Product §1] The Statbotics outage claim is drift, and it contradicts the research.** The document: "a single maintainer whose API returned 500s for ~4 weeks (2026-06-13 to 2026-07-11)." `00-strategy.md` says the opposite in as many words: "*Verified today: Statbotics has had zero commits since 2026-06-13 (~8 weeks, sole committer avgupta456) though the API itself is live and healthy — so the pitch is fragility and stalled maintenance, **never an ongoing outage***." The doc has converted a commit-activity gap into a fabricated four-week 500-error outage, then propagated it into Backend §1 ("literally its multi-week 2026 outage") and into `02-section-data.md` §1 ("one four-week outage"). This is the kind of claim that, when a Chief Delphi reader checks it in ten seconds, ends the project's credibility and poisons the "invited complement, not hostile fork" framing that Ledger's whole positioning depends on. **Fix:** replace every instance with the verified claim — stalled maintenance, narrowing upstream contract, and the separately-verified Championship-week "team not found" reports.

> **Editor's note — this finding is half right.** The reviewer read `00-strategy.md`'s phrase *"never an ongoing outage"* as denying that any outage occurred. It does not; it warns against implying the API is *currently* down. The fact-check in `01-gaps.md` independently confirms two real, still-open, never-answered outage tickets — #414 (2026-06-15, `HTTP 500 on all data endpoints`) and #418 (2026-07-18) — with the API healthy on 2026-08-07. So an outage claim is defensible; a *continuous four-week* outage claim is not, since it interpolates a duration between two unanswered tickets that the sources never establish. Corrected accordingly in [&sect;0 C-1](../DESIGN.md#0--normative-decisions-and-corrections) — and the accurate version is the stronger argument: nobody answered either ticket.

**27. [NFR-5] The advertising-congestion arithmetic is wrong by roughly 6×, and it's the sole justification for adaptive backoff.** "Three BLE advertising channels at ~3 ms occupancy means 400 devices at 1 Hz consume >100% of channel time." A legacy advertising PDU with a full 31-byte payload is 47 bytes total = **376 µs** at 1M PHY, transmitted once *per channel* on three **separate** channels. Per-channel load at 400 devices × 1 Hz is 400 × 376 µs = **150 ms/s ≈ 15% occupancy**, not >100%. Backoff is still worth having (collisions, scan-window overlap, scan responses, and 3,000 other people's phones), but the stated justification is arithmetically false and a reader who checks it will discount everything else in the table. **Fix:** correct the number, and re-justify adaptive advertising on collision probability and scanner-side connection storms — or defer it to v2 per open question 5, which is the right call.

**28. [NFR-6 / Persona table / Frontend §5] The Chromebook baseline is wrong.** "many 2016 units hit auto-update expiry on **Chrome 91**" and "the Chromebooks are past auto-update expiry (**Chrome ~91**)". The named reference device, the Acer C731 (Celeron N3060), reached AUE in **June 2022**, freezing at roughly **Chrome 102**, not 91. Chrome 91 corresponds to 2014–2015 hardware. The error is conservative, so nothing breaks — but it means the team is paying polyfill bundle weight for a floor that doesn't exist, and it's a fact a school IT mentor will correct in public. The more important, unstated consequence: the design's flagship rookie persona runs an unpatched browser four years past security support, which is where FR-3's key material lives. **Fix:** set the floor from a real survey (open question 16 already asks for this — do it in v0, it costs one forum post), and add an explicit statement about what hardware-backed key material means on an AUE'd device.

**29. [Backend §1 vs Frontend §1] Capacitor 7 vs Capacitor 8.** Backend picks "Capacitor 7"; Frontend picks "Capacitor 8 (current line; 8.5 shipped July 2026)". Capacitor 8 shipped December 2025 and 8.5 exists; Capacitor 9 has been in development since May 2026. Frontend is right, Backend is stale by a major. Worth noting the irony: Frontend rejects React Native for "the upgrade treadmill *is* the burden we are avoiding," and then picks a framework on an annual major cadence that will ship Capacitor 9 mid-2027 — inside the freeze window. **Fix:** Capacitor 8, pinned, everywhere; add a policy that major upgrades happen only in June–August, and put it in the freeze table.

**30. [Backend §8 / NFR-8 / SecOps §8-9] The $99 Apple fee — the one cost the document calls unavoidable and "stated honestly" — is waivable, and the doc didn't check.** Apple waives the Developer Program membership fee for nonprofit organizations, **accredited educational institutions**, and government entities in eligible countries including the US, provided the account distributes only free apps and has not signed the Paid Applications Agreement. This project is contractually barred from revenue by FIRST's ToU, so it qualifies by construction. Three separate sections build a narrative around "$99/year, all of it Apple, the one place succession cannot be made free." It also opens an option the document never considers in open questions 1, 7, or 27: an **accredited school district** can hold the account, which is exactly the entity every FRC team already sits inside. **Fix:** rewrite the cost tables to $0 recurring with a fee-waiver application as a P0 governance task (it still requires D-U-N-S + EIN, so the entity question survives — but the *cost* argument dissolves and the strongest sentence in Backend §8 becomes wrong).

**31. [SecOps §10] IRI is a July event, and by the document's own "today" it has already passed.** "land at offseason events (IRI, Chezy Champs, Battle of the Bay) in **Sept–Nov**." The Indiana Robotics Invitational runs in mid-July. Product §8 gets this right by naming only Chezy Champs and Bunnybots. The plan's v0 field-test window is 4–5 months from a document dated August 2026, against an App Store submission deadline of 2026-12-01, with the legal-entity question (open questions 1, 7, 27) unresolved and a D-U-N-S + 501(c)(3) formation lead time of months. **The critical path to iOS is entity formation, and it appears in no phase table.** **Fix:** drop IRI, and add "legal entity + D-U-N-S + Apple fee waiver filed" as an explicit P0 milestone with a date, because everything iOS depends on it.

**32. [Backend §3.1] COSE header label 6 is not `iat`.** The protected header is specified as `{1: -8, 4: bstr .size 8, 6: uint}` with 6 annotated as `iat`. In the COSE header parameter registry, label 6 is `counter signature` (RFC 8152, deprecated in RFC 9052) — `iat` is label 6 in the **CWT claims** registry, a different namespace. The stated reason for choosing COSE is "audited libraries" in five languages; a strict implementation will reject or misinterpret this. **Fix:** move `iat` into the payload (you already have `ts` and `hlc` there — it's redundant) or register it in the unprotected header with a private-use negative label.

**33. [Frontend §2.3] Concrete bugs in the shipped code sample.** (a) `db.envelope.where({ eventKey })` — `eventKey` is not a standalone index in the declared schema (indexes are `contentHash, sealedAt, [eventKey+matchKey+teamKey], [scoutId+matchKey], replicas`); Dexie will throw `SchemaError` at runtime. This is in `soleCopy`, which drives the entire staleness UI. (b) `replicas` increments on every `accepted` hash with no peer identity, so re-pushing to the same peer inflates it — and `replicas >= 2` is what the state machine calls "archived" and what the amber warning keys off. The one number the design says users must trust is the one that lies. (c) Nothing ever writes `state: 'archived'`. **Fix:** add an `eventKey` index; replace the counter with a `Set<peerId>` in a `replica` table and derive the count.

**34. [Frontend §9 / §7] Alliance Red fails WCAG 1.4.11 against the dark surface.** `--alliance-red #B3123B` (L ≈ 0.1033) on `--surface #0E1116` (L ≈ 0.00546) is **2.76:1** — below the 3:1 minimum for non-text UI components, in a section that claims AAA contrast on the scouting route and ships a deuteranopia CI gate. The red-vs-blue *separation* math (3.78:1) is correct; the red-vs-background math was never done. **Fix:** either lighten alliance red to ≥3:1 against `--surface` (breaking the 3.78:1 separation — you'll need to re-solve both constraints jointly) or mandate a 2 px `--fg`-adjacent border on every red badge, which the elevation token already implies. Add "each alliance fill vs each surface token" to the CI contrast matrix, not just fill-vs-fill.

**35. [SecOps §1] The design ships a minor-PII feature while claiming it holds none.** `scout_roster(scout_id, local_label)` with `local_label` = `'Ada'` is a name→behavioral-profile mapping, created by software this project ships, stored on a student's device, and read by the UI that surfaces CUSUM "asleep scout" alarms. The org holds none of it — true. But a district IT reviewer evaluating the APK sees a roster table with student names, and constraint 13's whole lesson is that *perceived* PII exposure is what barred California teams. The `WITH (courier_export = 'forbidden')` syntax is also Postgres table-options syntax that is invalid in SQLite and DuckDB, i.e. the venue pack's actual engines. **Fix:** don't ship the roster table. Ship `scout_id` + a user-chosen non-name label with an input filter, or leave the mapping entirely to paper and a whiteboard, and say so in the store listing. The enforcement test stays.

**36. [Frontend §4] The `photo` widget is either useless or a compliance hazard.** `contentMediaType: image/*` → camera capture, "Blob stays local; **never** enters a BLE envelope." Pit-scouting photos that never sync serve no purpose (the picklist laptop never sees them). If they *do* sync via USB, they're arbitrary camera captures from minors' phones in a venue full of minors, inside an opaque body Courier cannot inspect. **Fix:** cut the photo widget from v1. If it returns, route it over USB only, with an explicit on-capture warning and a size cap, and state the policy in the store listing.

**37. [Frontend §5 vs SecOps §6 vs Product §8] Ledger's "quarterly" rebuild is "weekly in the offseason."** FR-28: "CI-tested **quarterly**." SecOps §7: "A full rebuild of Ledger from L0 runs **weekly in the offseason**" — which is zero coverage during the six months that matter. Minor, but it's the succession guarantee. **Fix:** monthly, year-round, with the in-season run on a reduced season range if runner disk is the constraint.

**38. Machinery designed for no stated requirement.** Cut or defer each of these and name the requirement if you keep them: **L2CAP CoC** (defect 15); **the 16-byte HLC** — §4 states `rev` is the primary edit ordering and HLC is "only a tiebreak," while dedup is content-addressed, so a 16-byte-per-envelope field plus a clock-sync protocol buys a tiebreak that `record_id` lexicographic ordering already provides for free; **`enc: 2`** — exists solely for the optional relay that open question 11 suggests shouldn't ship; **the keyring PKI** (`repudiate`, `promote`, `link`, epochs, 2-of-N steward threshold, transparency log, Shamir 3-of-5) — a full trust hierarchy for ≤12 devices belonging to one high-school club, whose realistic adversary retreated to USB flash drives last June; **Pack Studio (S4)** — a drag-and-drop form authoring SPA with a lint pass is *half a scouting app*, directly contradicting §0's "we are explicitly not shipping a scouting app" and §1's thesis that building the app is the pedagogical good the customer is buying; **the 28-component design system, IBM Plex subsetting, and `@frc/a11y-tokens`** — a full design system for a product that ships no scouting UI (a11y-tokens is genuinely valuable and genuinely unowned, but it is a separate donation, not part of this build).

**39. Requirements with no design anywhere.** Coach Rivera's "**pre-match briefing 6 minutes before queuing**" is a named persona's core job-to-be-done and appears in no section — the 28-component inventory has no briefing surface, §6 covers picklists only, and no data path delivers opponent capability to a phone in the queue line. Ana's "**per-scout completeness report** at 18:00" is promised in journey (a) and designed nowhere. Sam's Week-0 scoring engine is v2 (2028), so the persona gets nothing in v1 — say so. Rookie 9xxx is the persona the architecture serves *worst*: ChromeOS is declared central-only (no advertising), so a rookie with only borrowed Chromebooks cannot form a mesh at all, needs USB-OTG adapters they don't have, and **cannot install a Capacitor app on a district-managed Chromebook without admin allowlisting** — a distribution problem no section addresses despite constraint 17. **Fix:** either design the briefing surface and the completeness report, or demote Rivera and Ana's report to non-goals. For rookies, add the managed-Chromebook install path (admin console allowlist instructions + a pure-web QR-only fallback that needs no install) as a first-class deliverable.

## (b) Top 5 additions required for completeness

1. **A normative wire-format spec, in one file, that every other section references.** One CDDL for the envelope, one dedup key (`record_id`), one signing construction (COSE_Sign1 Sig_structure, not "canonicalCbor(env)"), one canonical-CBOR profile, one reserved `schema_id` registry, one versioning rule for the format itself. Right now four sections describe four formats and the document has no single source of truth for the one artifact all three products share. Nothing should be built until this exists.

2. **The decoder story — or the deletion of everything that needs one.** `schema_id` → decoder resolution, a reference decoder generated from the same JSON Schema the form engine consumes, a fallback for teams with no decoder, and an explicit statement of what Ledger/Data §4/§5/§7 and Frontend §6/§10 do when `useDecoder()` returns nothing. This is the seam between "transport nobody has to agree on" and "analytics that require agreement," and the document currently pretends the seam isn't there.

3. **An offline official-results path.** How FMS alliance totals for matches played today reach a pit with no uplink, what the blend does before they arrive, and how the staleness UI communicates "your picklist is running unconstrained." Without this, journey (c) — the flagship user journey — does not work, and the constrained update that Data §4 calls "the piece nobody ships" still isn't shipped.

4. **A P0 measurement campaign report template, with the numbers currently asserted marked as unmeasured.** Connection setup time, iOS-peripheral sustained throughput under `updateValue` backpressure, iOS background advertising discoverability from Android, ChromeOS advertising capability, real advertising-channel occupancy at density, and the 20k-draw simulation wall-clock on an N3060. Eight of the twenty-seven open questions are measurement questions, and four NFRs are derived from unmeasured estimates. The document should ship with those cells visibly empty rather than filled with plausible numbers, because filled-in guesses will be treated as spec by whoever implements them.

5. **A January capacity plan with names against hours.** Every January this project owes, concurrently: Season Pack `1.0.0` in 72 h, `1.1.0` within 48 h of first breakdown, a TU watcher running all season with 24 h SLAs, `bridge_profiles.json` updates for four third-party apps whose QR column order changes, a form-generator update, and reconciliation-validator updates — all during the six weeks when every student maintainer is also building a robot, and all under a freeze policy that forbids shipping native code. State the hours, name the humans, and if the honest answer is "one person for 60 hours in three weeks," write that down, because that number is the actual product risk.

## (c) Verdict

**Not buildable as written. The thesis is right, the scope is roughly 4× what the named team can carry, and the internal contradictions are load-bearing rather than cosmetic.**

The strategic analysis is the strongest part of this document and it is genuinely correct: the transport problem is real, the rules reading is verified (E301 §14.3 exactly as quoted, no Bluetooth mention, R905 scoped to the operator console), the Web Bluetooth peripheral wall is real, and the "build beneath the rebuild" positioning correctly refuses the fight the community has repeatedly won against. If someone shipped only the Bridge — QR in, signed envelope out, BLE gossip, USB export, nothing else — it would be a good and possibly adopted piece of software.

That is not what this document proposes. It proposes a Rust protocol core with WASM/JNI/static-lib bindings, ~2,000 lines of claimed (realistically 6–10k) cross-platform native BLE including a peripheral role, L2CAP CoC, chunking with credit flow control, and iOS state restoration; a threshold-signature PKI with Shamir-split roots and a transparency log; a Go relay; a Go CLI; a Python/DuckDB ingestion pipeline; a 24-year archive; a novel Kalman rating; a novel picklist draft simulator; a benchmark whose headline metric open question 6 admits may be uncomputable; five frontend surfaces; a 28-component design system; a form authoring studio; an i18n system with RTL islands; and an annual 72-hour schema sprint that must not miss once, forever. The document's own success criteria say two of these tracks (the plugin, the benchmark) are likely to be killed. The remaining stack is still bigger than TBA, which has four trustees and 449 stars.

**Where it dies in year two:** not on adoption — the adopt-nothing Bridge is a genuinely good answer to that, and it is the strongest idea here. It dies in **January 2028**. The Season Pack sprint requires two rotating Schema Stewards, a Manual Watcher on a 24-hour SLA, plus four bridge-profile updates and a form-generator update, all inside a six-week window, staffed by volunteers who in year one were founding seniors and in year two are freshmen who inherited a Rust/Kotlin/Swift/Go/Python/TypeScript codebase with 2,000 lines of BLE native code they cannot debug without the eight-device lab. The document then writes its own execution: "**One miss is terminal.**" It has correctly identified the failure mode and staffed it with the least available people at the least available moment, then pre-committed to shutting down when they inevitably slip. Meanwhile the freeze policy makes native transport bugs found in Week 1 unfixable until June, which falsifies the "every in-season change is data, not code" claim for the one component that is neither.

**Second death, sooner:** the iOS critical path. Legal entity → D-U-N-S → Apple organization enrollment (→ fee waiver, which the document doesn't know exists) has a multi-month lead time, appears in no phase table, is the subject of three unresolved open questions, and gates a 2026-12-01 App Store submission from a document dated August 2026.

**What to actually do.** Cut to the Bridge plus Season Pack, and delete or defer the rest. Ship: QR ingest with `bridge_profiles.json`, one envelope format, BLE GATT gossip with range digests, USB mass-storage sneakernet, Android + ChromeOS only, no PKI beyond a per-mesh symmetric key and a QR pairing SAS, no relay, no L2CAP, no HLC, no encryption, no Pack Studio, no design system, no benchmark, no Ledger rating model. Ledger becomes exactly one thing: free current bulk Parquet with no account, which is the highest-feasibility item in `00-strategy.md` (8/10), is genuinely missing, requires no client, and is the only deliverable here that survives the founders leaving without anyone doing anything. Answer open questions 1/7/27 (entity + Apple waiver) before writing code, because they gate iOS and iOS is the dominant device class in the stands.

The one-sentence version: **this is three good products, one great strategy memo, and a build plan that a funded team of eight would find ambitious.**
