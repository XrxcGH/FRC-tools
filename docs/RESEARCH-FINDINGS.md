## Offline device-to-device sync is genuinely unsolved: venue WiFi is banned, QR is manual toil, web Bluetooth doesn't work, and the hardware answer costs $300–$930
[CONFIRMED / confidence high]

Confirmed, and the rules constraint is STRONGER than the candidate stated — but one important premise needs correcting.

I pulled the actual 2026 FRC Game Manual PDF and extracted the text. Section 14.3 Wireless Rules, page 153 of 166, rule E301: 'No wireless communication. Teams may not set up their own 802.11a/b/g/n/ac/ax/be wireless communication (e.g. access points or ad-hoc networks) in the venue.' with the blue box: 'A wireless hot spot created by a cellular device, camera, smart TV, etc. is considered an access point.' This is VENUE-WIDE, not merely 'near the field' as the candidate wrote. It also explicitly bans ad-hoc networks, which means a team-created local WiFi for WebRTC/LocalSend/PairDrop transfer is illegal — this is precisely why Viper's compliant answer is wired ethernet on battery, and therefore why it costs $930.

Viper hardware doc verified verbatim: 'The total cost of all the recommended hardware is around $930 (as of 2026), two thirds of which is the clients', 'Because WiFi hotspots are disallowed near the field, it is best to run this application with wired ethernet', 'It will need to run on batteries because there are almost never power outlets in the stands.' @stephen.ostermiller separately puts the server at ~$300 in-thread. @Turtles's hardware-failure report ('either due to hardware malfunction or too many people using cellular') is verbatim, as is the Chromebook/Mac/Windows/phone heterogeneity killing Windows Nearby Sharing. Maneuver quotes verbatim: @Hank_3620's 'Bluetooth is the #1 syncing method for an app going offline-first'; @ShinyShips's 'web Bluetooth APIs are not very useful for Maneuver... would require either wrapping in something like capacitor or a full rewrite as a native app'; 'I don't have TURN servers to connect to (these would cost me money and I currently only spend money developing this)'; @jake.s's 'syncing over the fountain codes and WIFI Sync don't work that well.' QRScout PWA refresh cliff verbatim from @Braden_Reed and @Ty_Tremblay's 'you've hit the limit of our browser based tech stack there.'

MATERIAL CORRECTION: Bluetooth is NOT banned in FRC. The manual's Bluetooth prohibition is R905, which applies only to the OPERATOR CONSOLE ('no other form of wireless communications shall be used to communicate to, from, or within the OPERATOR CONSOLE'), i.e. the drive team's control station — not scouts in the stands. This was resolved in-thread: @Turtles initially cited E301 as blocking Bluetooth, and @QwertyChouskie corrected him after checking the manual — 'So it seems Bluetooth is fair game in FRC. That's definitely much less restrictive than FTC's E301.' So the Bluetooth blocker is purely a browser-platform limitation (Web Bluetooth has no peripheral/GATT-server role and no iOS Safari support), NOT a rules limitation. The candidate got the conclusion right but for a partly wrong reason, and this matters because it means a Capacitor wrapper is legally viable.

ALSO UNMENTIONED: legal transports surfaced in the same thread that the candidate's 'every option fails' framing omits — USB tethering and Bluetooth tethering (both legal in FRC), @ShinyShips's own suggestion to 'connect bunch of devices via usb hub and cables, and just do JSON transfers', and @FletcherS7's observation that 802.11ad is not in E301's banned protocol list. @Ty_Tremblay also notes the refresh cliff has been 'a non factor' across 4 years of QRScout use, so that sub-claim is softer than presented.

**What exists today:** QRScout: 30 stars, last commit 2026-04-28, active, offline PWA, QR-only — the most reliable option but one human scan per device per match. ScoutingPASS: 77 stars (most-starred in the lane), last commit 2026-01-25, config-driven, QR-only. Maneuver: frc-maneuver.com HTTP 200, maneuver-core last commit 2026-06-01, 6k+ users in 2026, offline-first with no accounts — but its three software transports (QR, fountain codes, WebRTC WiFi sync) are reported broken by multiple named users and structurally blocked on TURN servers the solo dev cannot fund. Viper: 19 stars, last commit 2026-06-23, active, wired-ethernet Pi Zero server, ~$930 documented BOM — the only fully rules-compliant hands-off answer, and 3128 reports theirs failing at champs. Lovat Collection: 11 stars, last commit 2026-07-23, native iOS/Android (so native BT is reachable) but ships QR upload.

**Why nobody built it:** E301 forecloses the cheap answer. Any team-created 802.11 network — hotspot, ad-hoc, or otherwise — is a rules violation venue-wide, so the entire class of 'just run a local WiFi and sync over it' solutions is illegal, which is why the compliant answer (Viper) is wired and costs $930. The one legal, cheap, hands-off transport that remains is native Bluetooth — and that is unreachable from the PWA form factor every solo dev has chosen, because Web Bluetooth cannot act as a peripheral and iOS Safari does not implement it at all. Reaching it requires a Capacitor wrapper or native rewrite plus app-store distribution and maintenance across iOS, Android, ChromeOS and Windows — an ongoing burden no single student maintainer can carry, which is exactly why @ShinyShips deferred it to 'maybe... the offseason.' The constraint is not knowledge; it is cross-platform native maintenance labor with no funding.
